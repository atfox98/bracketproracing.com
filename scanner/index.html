Timeslip Scanner

The Timeslip Scanner app for iPhone reads a timeslip with the camera and sends it to Bracket Pro over Bluetooth. Run Entry opens with a new run filled in from the slip, with the weather for the time printed on it, ready for you to check and save. There's nothing to type, and it works without internet or Wi-Fi.

What you need

  • The Timeslip Scanner app on your phone. It's free, coming soon to the App Store and Google Play, with the full version (bought once, in the app) for scanning slips. You can test the connection before you buy.
  • Bracket Pro on your computer, with the timeslip scanner turned on in Settings → Timeslip scanner.
  • Bluetooth on that computer. Nearly every laptop has it. A desktop computer without it can use a small USB Bluetooth 5 adapter.

The first time the scanner starts, your computer asks whether Bracket Pro may use Bluetooth: allow it. A Timeslip scanner card then appears on Bracket Pro's Connect page, and the top of the window shows whether a phone is connected.

Pairing a phone

You pair each phone once:

  1. In Bracket Pro, click Pair a phone on the Timeslip scanner card. A QR code appears.
  2. In the phone app, give the phone a name (This phone's name), then tap Scan pairing code and point the camera at it.
  3. The phone connects, the window closes by itself, and the phone appears in the card's list.

From then on, the phone connects by itself whenever the app is open and Bracket Pro is running with the scanner turned on. Pair as many phones as you like (yours and a crew member's, say); they can all be connected at the same time.

Trying it before you buy

Before you buy the full version, check that your phone and computer work together. Pair the phone, then tap Test connection in the app. It sends a short message and a test photo (not a timeslip), and Bracket Pro shows Success! with the photo and how long it took to arrive.

If the photo doesn't arrive, move the phone closer to the computer and test again. When it works, buy the full version in the app to scan your slips.

Scanning a timeslip

Lay the slip flat, on something darker than the paper (a car seat or the trailer floor works), and tap Scan timeslip. Hold the phone over the slip: the camera finds its edges and takes the picture by itself. Tap Save, and the phone reads the slip and shows you the scan. Tap Use this scan to send it, or Scan again to go back to the camera. Already took a picture of the slip? Tap Choose from Photos.

On the computer:

  • Run Entry opens with a new run filled in from the slip: the round, reaction time, 60 ft, 330 ft, 594 ft, ET, MPH, dial-in, car number and lane. The date and time come from the slip, and the weather is the reading Bracket Pro logged nearest that time.
  • Notes say whether you won or lost and by how much, and Red light when your reaction time was negative.
  • The track is the one named on the slip, if it's one of your tracks, or your current track.
  • 1/4 mile slips are read too: the 1000 ft, ET and MPH, as well as the 1/8 ET and MPH.

Nothing is saved until you click Save run, so check the run first. See timeslip image shows the photo of the slip, so you can check a number against it. The photo stays with the run once you save it.

Checking a scanned run

Bracket Pro tells your lane from your car number, using the runs already in your logbook. If it can't tell (a new car, a test number for the day), it asks Which lane were you in? and remembers the answer for next time. To change the lane afterwards, click Left or Right next to Lane.

Fields Bracket Pro wasn't sure of have an amber outline: a smudged digit, a missing decimal point, or clocks that aren't in order down the track. Check those against the slip before you save. Slips are laid out differently at every track and by every timing system, so the first slips from a new track are worth a closer look.

Something read wrong? In the phone app, tap Report a misread under that slip. An email opens with the slip's photos and what was read already attached; add what was wrong and send it, so the slip reader can be fixed for everyone.
